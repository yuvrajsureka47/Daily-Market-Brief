US Strikes Iranian Rocket Launchers on Larak Island — Hormuz Risk Resets

Fed Chair Warsh Opens Door to Rate Hike at Jackson Hole

In his first Jackson Hole appearance as Fed Chair, Kevin Warsh said inflation "underlying trends" have not meaningfully improved, noting CPI at 3.4% and PCE at 3.7% — both well above the 2% target after 65 consecutive months above target. He stopped short of pre-committing to a September hike but called financial conditions "not broadly restrictive." Markets immediately repriced: CME FedWatch moved rate hike bets to above 60% for October–December. Why it matters: A Fed rate hike in a world already squeezed by oil price inflation would be a double tightening shock — higher rates AND higher energy costs — creating a stagflationary headwind for equities and credit.

China

China August Manufacturing PMI: 49.8 — Better Than Expected But Still Contracting

The National Bureau of Statistics released the August PMI at 49.8, beating the Reuters consensus of 49.6 but marking the second consecutive month of sub-50 contraction. July's reading was a sharp drop to 49.2 from June's 50.3. Weak domestic demand, a still-depressed property sector, and typhoon disruptions across southern China weighed on output. Non-manufacturing PMI remained flat at 49.0. Why it matters: The improvement is marginal, not a trend reversal. A contracting China — the world's largest commodity buyer — structurally reduces upward pressure on industrial metals but does nothing to contain oil, which is supply-shock driven. Expect Beijing to announce additional stimulus signals this week as the data lands on policymakers' desks.
