Nifty Falls for Fourth Consecutive Session, Settles at 22,600 — Crude and FII Outflows Dominate
The Nifty 50 declined 87.65 points (0.39%) to close at 22,600 on Friday, touching an intraday low of 22,372. The primary driver is elevated Brent crude above $116, which expands India's import bill and pressures the rupee. Key support now sits at 22,372.

DII Buying Continues to Cushion FII-Driven Selling Pressure in 2026
Despite FII selling of Rs 2.7 lakh crore in 2026, domestic institutional investors have absorbed over Rs 4.16 lakh crore, preventing deeper index corrections. SIP inflows remain structurally strong, underpinning the floor in Indian equities. However, this cushion has limits — a sustained crude oil shock could compress earnings for oil-import-dependent sectors and force a reassessment of forward earnings estimates across metals, infrastructure, and auto sectors.
Europe
ECB Rate Decision Due Thursday — Markets Price ~60% Chance of a Hike to 2.75%
The ECB's Governing Council meets October 28-29, with the decision published Thursday at 14:15 CET. Euro area inflation remains above target at 3.2% year-on-year. Two ECB sources told Reuters that a move was possible as early as October. A hike to 2.75% would push policy into mildly restrictive territory. This decision matters for global bond markets because a hawkish ECB alongside a held Fed would tighten global financial conditions simultaneously — negative for risk assets and emerging market flows.

Germany's Q3 GDP reading on Friday will clarify whether Europe's largest economy is in or near recession.
China
China Q3 GDP Grows 4.6% — Beats Estimates, Full-Year 4.5–5% Target Now Achievable
China's Q3 GDP came in at 4.6% year-on-year, above expectations, recovering from the 4.3% slowdown in Q2. For the first nine months of 2026, cumulative GDP growth stands at 4.6%. Beijing's state planner reaffirmed that more proactive macro policies will continue, including a national-level M&A fund and an AI industry scale target exceeding CNY 10 trillion. The better-than-expected print reduces near-term pressure for emergency stimulus and supports commodity demand, including oil and iron ore.
China PMI Data Due Saturday — Bellwether for November Macro Trajectory
Official PMI data for China releases Saturday. With Q3 GDP surprising on the upside but domestic demand structurally weak (fixed-asset investment fell 5.7% in H1), PMI will confirm whether the Q3 improvement is broadening or concentrated only in exports and high-value manufacturing. A manufacturing PMI above 50 would be constructive for commodity-linked assets globally, including Indian metals and mining stocks.

AI Watch
Infrastructure / Capex
Big Tech AI Capex on Track for $725B in 2026 — Up 77% from 2025
Meta, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon combined spent a record $130B on AI infrastructure in Q1 2026 alone. Meta has raised its 2026 capex to $135–$145B; Alphabet guided $180–$190B. The 2026 collective spend is projected to reach $725B, up from $410B in 2025 — a 77% increase. This is the single most important structural driver of data center construction, semiconductor demand, and energy consumption globally. Alphabet and Microsoft report Q3 earnings Wednesday — their 2027 capex guidance will determine whether this spending cycle is accelerating or plateauing.

Key Takeaways
01
This is a five central bank week. The Fed (Oct 28), Bank of Canada (Oct 28), ECB (Oct 29), and Bank of Japan (Oct 30) all decide within five days. A held Fed plus a hiking ECB is the most likely outcome — but the guidance language from Powell's press conference on Wednesday is the variable markets will price hardest. A hawkish hold could send yields back toward 5.36% and trigger another leg lower in equities.
02
The oil shock is the most underappreciated risk. Brent at $116 is not a short-term blip. It reflects structural supply constraints from Strait of Hormuz tensions that do not resolve overnight. For India specifically, this translates directly into a wider current account deficit, higher inflation prints, and reduced space for an RBI rate cut. The Rs 3 lakh crore FII outflow story is partly crude-driven and may not reverse until oil prices correct meaningfully.
03
Alphabet and Microsoft Q3 earnings on Wednesday are the market's real inflection test. S&P 500 earnings are running 30–32% above year-ago levels overall — a genuinely strong season. But markets are punishing capex surprises (Meta fell 6% despite a beat). If Alphabet or Microsoft announces 2027 AI capex materially above consensus, the question becomes whether AI monetization is growing fast enough to justify the spend. The answer will determine whether the Nasdaq's current support at 25,900 holds or breaks.
04
DII resilience is buying time for Indian equities — not fixing the macro problem. DIIs have absorbed Rs 4.16 lakh crore against FII selling of Rs 2.7 lakh crore this year. This is structurally impressive and reflects the maturity of domestic retail participation through SIPs. However, if crude stays above $110 into Q4, corporate margins in autos, aviation, cement, and industrials will compress, and forward earnings estimates will need to be revised lower. Watch the Nifty 22,372 support closely — a sustained break opens a move toward 22,180.
05
The AI capex supercycle is intact but entering a scrutiny phase. $725B of planned 2026 AI infrastructure spend is historically unprecedented. The market is no longer rewarding spending announcements blindly — it wants to see monetization evidence. Microsoft's AI business crossed $37B annualized revenue run rate in Q3 (up 123%), and Alphabet's Gemini API generates ~$15B annually. The companies that can show the revenue-to-capex ratio improving will outperform those that cannot into year-end.
