🌐 Global Macro
🇺🇸 United States
Mega-cap earnings blitz: Meta and Tesla report tonight after SpaceX beat yesterday
SpaceX crushed Q3 estimates — EPS of $0.35 vs. $0.10 consensus — on revenue of $14.58 billion, up 161% year-over-year, driven by Starlink and data centre capacity rented to AI firms including Anthropic and Google. Meta (revenue est. $63.3B) and Tesla (revenue est. $27.9B) report after today's close. The stakes are high: the market wants proof that $130–145B of Meta capex earns a real return, and Tesla's declining EPS (est. $0.45, down 10% YoY) puts automotive margins squarely in focus.
Why it matters:
These three names represent the broadest test yet of whether AI capital expenditure translates into revenue. The direction of chip stocks, cloud sentiment, and the broader Nasdaq into year-end rides on these calls.
Inflation breadth wider than energy alone — services and core goods both accelerate
September CPI supercore — core services ex-housing — rose 0.42% month-on-month and 3.13% year-on-year, suggesting a price floor closer to 3% than the Fed's 2% target. Core goods prices also accelerated, making this a supply-and-demand inflation problem, not a pure energy story. The US 10-year yield holds at 5.09–5.10%, keeping financial conditions tight ahead of the Oct 28 Fed decision.
Why it matters:
A higher-for-longer rate environment suppresses growth multiples for tech, tightens credit for overleveraged AI infrastructure firms like CoreWeave, and delays any pivot narrative the equity market might price in.
South Korean AI IPO flops 30% at debut, triggering Asian chip selloff
Korean AI firm Elice fell over 30% on its Seoul debut, dragging the benchmark index down 3.7% to its lowest close since April 7. SK Hynix, SanDisk, and Marvell Technology fell 2.5%–4% in US pre-market. Chip stocks entered correction territory (down 10% from highs) after an eight-session losing streak, though Monday's partial rebound has not reversed the trend.
Why it matters:
Seoul's reaction function acts as a real-time stress test for AI infrastructure valuations globally. A failed AI IPO signals that institutional capital is beginning to demand cash flow proof, not just narrative.
🇮🇳 India
Jio Platforms IPO opens today — India's largest-ever listing at up to $3.8 billion
Reliance Industries' digital arm Jio Platforms opens for subscription today (Oct 21–23), priced at Rs 1,065–1,119 per share (top-end raises ~$3.2–3.8B), surpassing Hyundai Motor India's 2024 record of $2.9B. The fresh issue proceeds largely repay Rs 27,500 crore of Jio Infocomm debt. Anchor bidding was completed on Monday; both FII and DII feedback has been described as strong. Jio handles 60% of India's mobile data traffic with 530 million subscribers, with AI and cloud operations as the growth thesis.
Why it matters:
A record Indian IPO opening on a down-market day is a stress test for domestic liquidity. If oversubscribed, it signals DII firepower remains intact. It also draws capital away from secondary markets in the short window, partially explaining Monday's 1.11% Nifty decline.
Nifty 50 fell 1.11% on Monday, GIFT Nifty pointing to gap-up open today
Nifty 50 closed at 22,888.95 on Monday (Oct 19), losing 257 points as heavyweights sold off amid high oil prices. Sensex shed 876 points (-1.17%) to 73,853, marking a 12.46% year-to-date decline. However, GIFT Nifty was up over 100 points at 23,012 this morning (Oct 21), tracking Asian market gains and suggesting a recovery open for Wednesday's session.
Why it matters:
India's equity market is navigating a difficult macro environment — Brent near $110/bbl compresses margins for energy-importing sectors, while Jio IPO sucks near-term liquidity from the secondary market. The recovery in GIFT Nifty is fragile and data-dependent.
Elevated crude oil prices weighing on India's current account and inflation outlook
Brent crude approaching $110/barrel per Business Standard live updates creates twin pressure on India: import bill widens, rupee faces depreciation risk, and domestic fuel inflation builds. The RBI's space to cut rates narrows as energy-driven CPI feeds through. Indian energy and refining stocks remain a watch item given Reliance Industries' dual exposure as refiner-retailer.
Why it matters:
India imports ~85% of its crude requirement. At $110 Brent, the fiscal math of fuel subsidies and current account deficit management becomes significantly more complex heading into Q4.
🇪🇺 Europe
ECB's Lane speaks in Shanghai today — October hold expected, December hike still live
ECB Chief Economist Philip Lane joins a closed-door panel in Shanghai at 04:15 GMT. The ECB raised rates twice this year (June and September) to 2.50%. September Eurozone CPI came in at 3.8% — the highest since September 2023 — with energy inflation at 18.8% year-on-year. Markets expect a hold on October 29, but the December meeting remains live for a third hike, with energy and wage data as the key inputs. The October 20 GDP revision from Eurostat also informs the decision calculus.
Why it matters:
Lane's speech, even if structural in theme, will be parsed for any signal that energy inflation is spreading into services and wage dynamics. A hint at December tightening would pressure European bond spreads and weigh on peripheral sovereigns like France.
France sovereign credit rating decision due Friday — bond market under pressure
Moody's France sovereign credit rating decision is scheduled for Friday (Oct 23). BlackRock's latest weekly commentary identified French bond turmoil as a lesson in selectivity, noting that higher sovereign yields do not always reflect compensated risk. French OAT spreads over German Bunds have widened amid fiscal concerns and energy cost headwinds to the industrial sector.
Why it matters (Geopolitical Risk):
A downgrade or negative outlook from Moody's would amplify French sovereign spreads, tighten credit conditions across French banks, and test ECB's credibility in managing both inflation and financial stability simultaneously.
🇨🇳 China
China's growth momentum fades in H2 2026 despite solid H1 start
After a strong start to 2026, China's economy surprised to the downside in the June quarter. Weak domestic demand persists, the property market downturn continues, and adverse weather events compounded soft survey data. Industrial output and exports remain the primary support for GDP, though the US Supreme Court ruling in February — striking down many Trump-era tariffs as unlawful — has reduced one major headwind. China targets 4.5%–5% GDP growth for 2026, the first year of its 15th Five-Year Plan.
Why it matters:
China's loss of momentum in Q2–Q3 2026 means it cannot act as a global demand buffer the way it did in earlier cycles. Weak Chinese commodity demand provides a partial offset to Middle East supply risk in oil markets — but slowing Chinese industrial output also signals reduced demand for semiconductors and industrial machinery, relevant for Korea and Taiwan.
Chinese equities underperform, semiconductor and AI-related shares weigh on benchmarks
Chinese mainland markets have underperformed Hong Kong over the past week, with selling in semiconductor and AI-related stocks driving declines. Foreign investor selling has added to volatility, extending a broader retreat from Asian financial assets as global borrowing costs rise and uncertainty around the US rate path continues.
Why it matters:
China's AI sector had been one of the few areas attracting risk capital. Sustained institutional selling signals that the global AI valuation correction is now crossing into Chinese technology equities — potentially pressuring the few remaining momentum plays in the region.

NextDC shares (Australia) tank 40% after delays to Melbourne data centre
Australia-listed data centre operator NextDC fell 40% after returning from a trading halt, following disclosure of significant delays to a major Melbourne data centre project. The ABC Markets Live blog flagged this as intensifying concerns about whether AI infrastructure investment in Australia can generate sufficient returns on the timeline promised to investors.
Why it matters:
A 40% single-day decline in a data centre operator mirrors CoreWeave's pressure in the US. The pattern is becoming global — AI infrastructure stocks that cannot demonstrate concrete revenue timelines are being sold hard. This is a direct read-across to the AI capex debate surrounding Meta and SpaceX tonight.

🤖 AI Watch
Earnings / Infrastructure
SpaceX beats Q3 estimates with 161% revenue growth — AI compute business emerging as primary engine
SpaceX reported EPS of $0.35 (vs. $0.10 estimate) on revenue of $14.58 billion, up 161% year-on-year. The company ended Q3 with 1.7 gigawatts of nameplate compute capacity. Crucially, SpaceX's AI business is structured as a data centre rental model — renting capacity to Anthropic, Google, and Reflection AI — making it closer to a cloud hyperscaler than an AI lab. Q3 capex was $19.6 billion, with Q4 guidance at $35–40 billion. Shares rose 8% in after-hours to $159.70. A large lock-up release follows earnings, however, giving early investors a window to sell.
Regulation
EU AI Act transparency obligations (Article 50) now in force — compliance burden shifts to enterprises
As confirmed in Stephenson Harwood's October 2026 Neural Network, Article 50 obligations under the EU AI Act are now fully in force. Providers and deployers of AI systems must comply with transparency requirements. Separately, the UK Government published its AI Risk Management Toolkit, and Parliament is considering regulation of AI-generated digital replicas. China's Supreme People's Court released guidance on AI tort liability, establishing a framework for assessing when an AI product is "unreasonably dangerous." These regulatory moves create compliance costs for enterprises deploying AI in regulated industries across three major jurisdictions simultaneously.

💡 Key Takeaways
01
The AI capex credibility crisis is the macro story of this earnings season. SpaceX's beat is encouraging for bulls, but the market is now demanding proof from Meta ($130–145B capex) and Tesla before it re-rates the AI trade higher. A mixed result tonight — beat on revenue, miss on guidance or FCF — could extend the Nasdaq's correction beyond chip stocks into the broader hyperscaler complex.
02
Oil above $100/bbl is a central bank trap. WTI near $100 and Brent near $110 forces the Fed and ECB to stay restrictive even as growth data softens. The US supercore at 3.13% YoY and Eurozone energy inflation at 18.8% YoY mean neither central bank has room to pivot. Watch the Oct 28 Fed and Oct 29 ECB decisions — both are likely holds, but the tone on December will set the trajectory for rates through year-end.
03
Jio Platforms IPO opening today is a DII liquidity test for India. India's primary market has raised over Rs 1.1 lakh crore across 96 IPOs in 2026. Jio at Rs 30,000+ crore is a sizable capital call. If oversubscribed heavily, it validates domestic institutional firepower and foreign interest. If subscription is muted, it signals the secondary market selloff has transferred into primary market caution — with implications for the 250+ companies in the IPO pipeline.
04
Gold outperforming Bitcoin in 2026 is a regime signal, not noise. Gold at $4,142 near all-time highs while Bitcoin sits at $81,590 — well below its $126,000 peak — reflects a flight to genuine store-of-value assets amid elevated bond yields and geopolitical risk. When gold grabs alpha at this pace, it historically precedes a broader risk-asset repricing. Watch the BTC/Gold ratio: currently ~19x, well below cycle highs.
05
Next 24–72 hours: three events to watch. (1) Meta and Tesla earnings after US close today — specifically Meta FCF and Tesla margin trajectory. (2) Jio Platforms subscription data as it rolls in — a proxy for India's appetite for digital and AI-adjacent equity. (3) Moody's France sovereign rating decision on Friday — a potential catalyst for European bond spread widening and ECB policy complexity heading into the Oct 29 meeting.
