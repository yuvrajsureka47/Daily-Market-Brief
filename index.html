United States

Context · Fri 20 Nov close, 4:00 PM ET

S&P 500 and Dow fall into correction; Nasdaq erases its 2026 gain

The S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, its seventh straight loss and a drop of 8.6% on the week, its worst since April 2025. The Dow fell 1.93% to 48,147.33 (8.0% lower on the week, its worst since March 2020), the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73 (10.0% lower on the week) and the Nasdaq 100 fell 5.61% to 25,028.98. The Russell 2000 fell 2.8% to 2,581.79, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 10.7%, and the VIX closed at 35.89, up from 19.30 a week earlier. Only about 123 S&P 500 stocks rose, and staples were the only sector higher. Why it matters: both the S&P 500 and Dow closed more than 10% below their records (October 6 and August 5), the usual definition of a correction, and the Nasdaq is now down 2.7% for 2026. The S&P 500 is still up 0.9% for the year.

Context · Wed 18 to Fri 20 Nov

Fed minutes and Waller keep a December hike priced, with a 50 bp debate

Minutes of the October 27 to 28 meeting showed more officials wanted a hike than the 10 to 2 vote suggested. Fed Governor Christopher Waller said on November 18 that the Fed may need to “shock people” and go 50 bp (basis points, 0.50%) in December unless it clearly signals another hike for January, and he is the only governor publicly backing 50 bp. Mid week readings put the chance of a 25 bp December hike at about 90% to 97% and 50 bp at 3% to 10%. The 10 year Treasury yield fell for a fifth straight session to 4.91% on Friday, a flight to safety, and is down about 24 bp on the week. Why it matters: yields fell with stocks, but the policy rate (3.75% to 4.00% after September’s hike) is still headed up. The decision comes December 9 at 2:00 PM ET, and October PCE inflation on Wednesday is the last major reading before it.

Fresh · Fri 20 to Sat 21 Nov

Tanker strikes at Hormuz and Houthi missiles toward Riyadh, with no deal in sight

UKMTO, the UK maritime monitor, said two tankers were struck by unknown projectiles early Saturday while leaving the Strait of Hormuz, one in the stern and one in the engine room, with no casualties reported. Iranian state media separately said a tanker caught fire after hitting a naval mine east of the strait, which is an Iranian claim. The Saudi led coalition said it downed three Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh. President Trump said Friday that Iran’s terms for a deal are unacceptable, a Qatari delegation held talks in Tehran Friday night, and Iran’s foreign minister said its 48 hour proposal to reopen Hormuz remains on the table. Why it matters: Brent settled Friday at $92.57 and WTI at $83.98, both on a third weekly decline, and oil futures reopen Sunday evening, so the first reaction to these weekend events has not been priced yet.

Context · Fri 20 Nov, provisional exchange data

FIIs buy ₹10,308 crore in a day as domestic funds sell

FIIs (foreign institutional investors) were net buyers of ₹10,308.42 crore on Friday and DIIs (domestic institutional investors) net sellers of ₹4,859.8 crore, per provisional BSE data, with Quantsapp’s rounded figures matching. For the week FIIs bought ₹32,647 crore and DIIs sold ₹12,698 crore. Quantsapp counts 15 straight sessions of net FII buying, and FPIs have reportedly put a record $12.21 billion into Indian equities in November after more than $60 billion of outflows between September 2024 and October 2026. Why it matters: foreign inflows, not domestic ones, are driving the rally, so it is exposed if the global dollar liquidity squeeze tightens. Provisional figures can be revised.

Chips

Nvidia falls 19% in three sessions despite record results

Context · Results Tue 17 Nov; stock Fri 20 Nov close
Nvidia reported fiscal Q3 revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year ago (guidance was $108 billion), with data center revenue of $107.6 billion and GAAP EPS of $2.70. It guided to about $130 billion for the January quarter, above the roughly $125 billion FactSet consensus. The stock still fell from $155.46 on results day to $125.53 on Friday, including a 14.01% drop on Friday after Reuters reported SEC enforcement staff are examining possible accounting issues. Reuters noted that investigations are not evidence of wrongdoing and many close without action. A California Attorney General subpoena (Thursday) over whether chips reached China and whether investors were misled, and a board investigation after CEO Jensen Huang’s November 12 resignation, add to the pressure. Nvidia lost nearly $900 billion in market value this week and is now fourth behind Apple, Microsoft and Alphabet (CNBC). Why it matters: the business is growing, so the sell off is about regulatory risk, AI financing and leadership, which is why a strong report did not stop it.
