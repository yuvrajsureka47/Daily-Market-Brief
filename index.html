China's rare earth export controls creating structural pressure on European industry
An ECB working paper warns that China's partial export restrictions on rare earths are acting as an adverse supply shock on European downstream industries. Because these inputs feed into a broad range of sectors and cannot be substituted quickly, production constraints and input cost inflation are propagating through eurozone supply chains. The impact roughly doubles when cross-input substitution is limited, which is the current situation given China's dominant position in rare earth supply.

This accelerates enterprise adoption timelines but compresses margins for AI-native companies. The real beneficiaries are now hyperscalers with proprietary data and companies that can embed AI into defensible workflows — not those whose product is the model itself.

A weak print (below 130K) would kill the September hike narrative outright, push 10Y yields below 4.5%, and give equity bulls another leg up. A strong print above 200K brings the hawks back and re-introduces rate risk into a market that is currently celebrating.
