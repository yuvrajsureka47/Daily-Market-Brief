June NFP Comes in at 57,000 — Labor Market Softening Sharply
The US economy added only 57,000 jobs in June, the weakest print in four months and far below the 110,000 consensus forecast. Prior months were also revised lower. The unemployment rate dipped to 4.2%, but that reflected a drop in labor force participation to its lowest since 2021 — a bearish signal, not a bullish one. Markets have repriced Fed rate hike odds for September down to roughly 50% from 75% pre-report. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has abandoned forward guidance entirely, which means Wednesday's FOMC minutes become unusually important for reading the policy drift.
Section 122 Tariff Expiry on July 24 — Legal Uncertainty Compounds Market Risk
The 10% blanket tariff imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act is set to expire on July 24. However, a May 7 ruling by the US Court of International Trade found the administration exceeded its legal authority in imposing this surcharge. The ruling does not immediately remove tariff exposure while appeals proceed, but it adds a binary risk event. If the tariff is not extended and the legal challenge succeeds, import costs fall across the board — a potentially disinflationary shock. Equities, especially consumer discretionary and retail, may reprice on resolution either way.
ISM Services PMI Signals Modest Slowdown, But Price Pressures Ease

The combination of weaker labor and softer price pressures gives the Fed a narrow argument against hiking in July, but September remains a live meeting depending on what the July CPI print shows on July 14.

India Current Account Swings to $4.7B Surplus in April 2026
India recorded a current account surplus of $4.7 billion in April 2026, reversing a $4.8 billion deficit from a year ago, according to preliminary RBI data. The improvement was driven by stronger services exports of $18.6 billion and surging remittances of $16 billion — nearly double the $9.4 billion seen a year earlier. FDI more than doubled to $11.4 billion. However, net FPI outflows rose to $8.7 billion, and banking capital turned negative. The rupee is hovering around 95.4 per dollar — still down 11% over the past 12 months — but the current account swing is structurally positive for medium-term balance of payments stability.
RBI Holds Repo at 5.25% While Relaxing NRI Deposit Rates to Support Flows
The RBI has temporarily removed the interest rate ceiling on 3-to-5-year FCNR(B) deposits through September 30, effective June 17. This allows banks to offer competitive rates to NRIs and attract foreign currency inflows — a tactical move to shore up the capital account while the repo rate stays on hold. Weighted average lending rates on fresh rupee loans have risen 10 bps to 8.5%, reflecting robust credit demand. The RBI is managing a difficult balance: holding rates steady to support growth, while deploying capital-account tools to defend the rupee and improve external balances.

The European Central Bank's June projections reflect a difficult environment: the eurozone remains stuck with weak sentiment, rate hikes weighing on growth, and shrinking consumer purchasing power. ECB President Lagarde has stated that while she welcomes the Iran ceasefire, the central bank remains vigilant about second-round inflation effects. European equities are underperforming US counterparts as a result of this policy divergence and weaker growth momentum.

Japan's yen at its weakest level in roughly 40 years is a slow-moving but potentially explosive risk. A surprise BOJ rate hike or Ministry of Finance intervention at or near the July 30-31 meeting could trigger a rapid unwind of yen-funded carry trades — where investors borrowed cheaply in yen to buy higher-yielding assets globally. A carry trade unwind forces selling of US equities, EM bonds, and high-yield assets simultaneously. The 2024 carry unwind erased 10-15% from global indices in a matter of days. This is the same setup, at higher leverage and a weaker yen starting point.
