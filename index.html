United States

Fresh · Wed 25 Nov close, 4:00 PM ET

The Dow erases a 1,100 point loss as stocks rebound late before Thanksgiving

The Dow rose 164.25 points (0.33%) to 50,182.95, its second straight close above 50,000, after being down about 1,100 points at its low. The S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,182.78 after dropping as much as 3.2%, the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62% to 23,585.80 after dropping as much as 4.6%, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 0.64% to 26,046.90 after briefly touching 20% below its October peak, the usual definition of a bear market. The Russell 2000 rose 0.71% to 2,704.06, and the VIX rose 7.32% to 28.29. Nvidia closed down 3.91% at $149.30 after being down 8.7% at its low. Why it matters: about three quarters of S&P 500 stocks rose, so the rebound was broad, but it came on thin pre holiday volume and the intraday swings show how fragile sentiment is.

Fresh · Wed 25 Nov, 8:30 AM ET releases

Hot core inflation and 4.3% growth leave a December Fed hike fully priced

October PCE inflation (the Fed’s preferred gauge) rose 0.3% on the month and 3.8% from a year earlier, up from 3.7% and in line with forecasts. Core PCE rose 3.3% from a year earlier, above the 3.2% forecast and a three year high. Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3% annualised from 3.4%, the fastest in three years. Markets see a December Fed hike as fully priced according to CME FedWatch (Reuters), and other trackers show about 97% to 98%, with the Fed having last raised rates in September. The 10 year Treasury yield fell about 6 bp (basis points, 0.01% each) to 4.85% and the 2 year to about 4.63%, after dipping below 4.80% during the session as investors bought bonds for protection. Why it matters: strong growth plus sticky inflation gives the Fed room to keep tightening, and yields fell with stocks, a safety bid and not relief. The Fed meets December 8 to 9.

Fresh · Wed 25 Nov, USTR announcement

The US proposes new excess capacity tariffs, but none take effect before late January

The US Trade Representative proposed Section 301 tariffs on 14 of the 16 economies it investigated for “structural excess capacity.” China faces up to 20% (up to 40% if its trade truce unravels), the EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland would stay at their 15% deal caps, India faces up to 25% or 17% if it concludes a trade deal, and Vietnam up to 35%. Public comments are due January 5, 2027, hearings start January 25, and no effective date has been set. Why it matters: these are proposals for comment, not tariffs in force, which is why stocks recovered after the first drop. Earlier pre announcement reports of “16 countries” were wrong: 16 were investigated, 14 were named.

Why it matters: Brent had been down to about $89.93 earlier on Wednesday on surplus expectations, so the attack claims are what reversed it. OPEC+ talks are due November 29.
