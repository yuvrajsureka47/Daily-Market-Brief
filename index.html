04 Key Takeaways
01
The world is entering a rare, synchronized global tightening cycle simultaneously across three major central banks — the Fed just hiked, the BoJ is set to hike at its September 17 to 18 meeting, and the ECB is already moving. Every additional hike across these three institutions collectively tightens global liquidity, compresses risk-asset valuations, and raises the bar for sustained equity rallies. Watch for cross-border capital flow reversals — particularly from carry trades that depended on divergence between BoJ and other central banks. The yen is likely to strengthen materially from here.
02
Oil at $107 Brent with two major export routes under attack — Strait of Hormuz and Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline — is not a temporary spike. The IEA's September Oil Market Report flags a 5.7 million barrels per day supply shortfall for 2026, roughly 6% of global output. If the pipeline remains offline and Hormuz traffic stays constrained, energy-importing economies face a sustained cost shock that central banks cannot solve with rate hikes alone. For India specifically, this is an inflation, fiscal, and currency problem in one — the worst combination heading into a tighter global liquidity environment.
03
The 10-year US Treasury at 5% is the most important number in global finance right now. This level makes the risk-free rate genuinely competitive with equity earnings yields for the first time since 2007. Historically, a sustained 5% yield has preceded meaningful valuation compression in equities — particularly for growth stocks with long-duration cash flows. The market's relative resilience today (with futures recovering) is partly a positioning story, but any additional yield move toward 5.5% would force institutional asset allocation shifts away from equities at scale.
04
China's consumer remains the missing engine. With retail sales growing below 1% and fixed-asset investment declining, Beijing cannot generate the demand-pull that would absorb global supply and stabilize commodity markets outside of oil. The PBOC's deliberate restraint — keeping rate cut ammunition in reserve — signals confidence that conditions have not deteriorated enough for a major bazooka, but each month of subdued consumption erodes the government's room to reach its 5% GDP target. If China announces meaningful fiscal stimulus by October, it would be a significant positive catalyst for EM equities, Asian credit, and metals.
