Fresh · Tue 24 Nov close, 4:00 PM ET

The Nvidia rebound fades in one day as chips plunge, while the Dow closes above 50,000

The S&P 500 fell 0.50% to 7,201.09 (its eighth loss in nine sessions), the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.48% to 23,733.54 and the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.99% to 26,214.17, while the Dow rose 0.85% to 50,018.70 and the Russell 2000 rose 0.57% to 2,684.94. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 7.88% to 8,832.50, with all 30 members lower. Nvidia fell 5.86% to $155.38 (intraday low $150.50) a day after its 31.48% jump, Micron fell 10.97% on fears of slower high bandwidth memory demand, and AMD fell 8.86%. Why it matters: about 346 S&P 500 stocks rose against 154 that fell, and the equal weight S&P 500 rose 0.54%, so this was a chip and megacap tech selloff, not a broad one. The Conference Board’s consumer confidence index jumped 11.4 points to 103.3, far above the 92.2 to 92.5 forecast and its highest since January 2025.

A New York Fed official backed a December rate hike on Tuesday, and the FOMC minutes released November 18 showed almost all officials judged a further increase likely appropriate. Governor Waller has floated a half point move, while Treasury Secretary Bessent has warned against overtightening, and the Fed meets December 8 to 9. The 10 year Treasury yield closed Tuesday near 4.90% (the ^TNX index quote was 4.904%) and the 2 year near 4.67%. Today at 8:30 AM ET (13:30 GMT, 19:00 IST) the BEA publishes October PCE inflation, with core expected up 0.3% on the month and about 3.2% from a year earlier (3.1% in September), together with the second estimate of Q3 GDP (advance estimate 3.4%) and jobless claims. Why it matters: this is the last major inflation reading before the December decision, and strong consumer data raises the risk of a hot print, not a soft one.
