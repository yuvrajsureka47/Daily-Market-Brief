Global Macro

United States

Context · Fri 20 Nov close, 4:00 PM ET

Wall Street’s worst week since April 2025 ends in a rout, with the S&P 500 and Dow in correction

The S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, its seventh straight loss and longest losing streak since February 2020, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73, turning negative for 2026, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 5.61% to 25,028.98 after touching bear market territory during the session. The Dow fell 1.93% to 48,147.33 and the Russell 2000 fell 2.76% to 2,581.79. For the week the S&P 500 lost 8.6%, the Nasdaq 10.0% and the Russell 2000 7.5%. The S&P 500 and Dow closed more than 10% below their records of October 6 and August 5. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 10.7%, and the VIX volatility gauge closed at 35.89, its highest close since April 2025. Why it matters: only 123 S&P 500 stocks rose against 376 that fell, and only consumer staples gained, so the equal weight S&P 500 fell 1.98%. This was no longer a rotation within tech. The selling reached the whole market.

US futures and gold bounce early, but futures are not cash prices

At 11:44 AM Sydney time (00:49 GMT) S&P 500 futures were up about 2.0% and Nasdaq 100 futures up about 2.8% (MarketIndex), after Reuters had reported gains of roughly 1% and 2% earlier in the session. Spot gold rose 1.1% to $3,577.06 an ounce and silver 2.2% to $42.11, Brent fell 0.9% to $91.78 and WTI fell 0.9% to $82.88 (Bloomberg via MarketIndex). Why it matters: gold fell about 9% last week alongside stocks, which fits forced selling to raise cash, not a haven bid, so its bounce alongside futures hints that selling eased. Context · Minutes of Wed 18 Nov; Fri 20 Nov comments

The Fed leans toward more hikes even as stocks fall

Minutes of the October 27 to 28 meeting, released November 18, showed almost all participants saw further rate increases as likely, and a couple said a move larger than 25 bp (basis points, 0.01% each) could warrant consideration in December. The Fed held at 3.75% to 4.00% in October in a 10 to 2 vote, with Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissenting in favour of a hike. On Friday Governor Waller said on CNBC he is very worried inflation is broadening and the Fed needs to act more aggressively, and Cook said one hike may not be enough. The next decision is December 9. The 10 year Treasury yield closed Friday at 4.912%, from 5.154% a week earlier, its biggest weekly drop since early April 2025. Why it matters: bonds rallied as stocks fell, but CME FedWatch priced above a 97% chance of at least a 25 bp hike in the week’s final days (Kiplinger). On Wednesday, October PCE inflation (the Fed’s preferred gauge) is the last big data point, with core PCE expected at 3.2% from a year earlier.

Accounting Probe

Nvidia falls 14% on a reported SEC probe, after a strong quarter

Context · Reuters, Fri 20 Nov; results Tue 17 Nov
Reuters reported, citing two people briefed on the matter, that SEC enforcement staff are scrutinising potential accounting issues at Nvidia. Reuters could not determine which issues or when the inquiry began, the SEC declined to comment, and Nvidia did not immediately respond. Nvidia closed at $125.53, down 14.01%, which is about 19% below its close of $155.46 on results day and about 23% below the prior Friday. On November 17 Nvidia reported fiscal Q3 revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, and guided Q4 revenue to $126 billion to $134 billion against a consensus near $125 billion, assuming no China data center sales, with gross margin guided to 71% to 72% against 74.8% in Q3. Nvidia has said it will file its 10-Q (quarterly report) in the coming weeks, tied to its board’s independent investigation. Why it matters: strong demand no longer protects the stock, because the debate has moved to accounting, margins and leadership, and the sell off spread to the whole chip chain.
