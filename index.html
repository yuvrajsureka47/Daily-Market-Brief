July CPI Released Today — Market Watches for Inflation Trajectory
The Bureau of Labor Statistics published its July CPI report at 8:30 AM ET today. Consensus heading in was for headline CPI to print +0.1% month-on-month and +3.4% year-on-year, with core CPI (which strips out food and energy) expected at +0.32% month-on-month. June's data had showed headline CPI fall 0.4% month-on-month — the largest single-month decline since April 2020 — driven entirely by a 9.7% crash in gasoline prices, while core remained unchanged. The July print matters because oil has surged back in August, and any upside surprise to inflation today would nearly lock in a September Fed rate hike. As of Tuesday, markets were pricing a 51% probability of a 25 basis point Fed hike in September — up from 44% the day before.
July Jobs Surprise Complicates the Fed's Path
Non-farm payrolls unexpectedly declined by 23,000 in July, with significant downward revisions to May and June data, signalling meaningful labour market softening. This initially pulled the 10-year Treasury yield down to 4.61% on Friday. But the subsequent surge in oil prices reversed that move, with the 10-year yield climbing back to 4.73% by Tuesday, its highest level since January. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack stated publicly that multiple rate increases may be necessary to return inflation to the 2% target. The conflict between weak employment and elevated energy-driven inflation puts the Fed in a genuine bind entering the September meeting.
