United States

Fresh · Fri 20 Nov close, 4:00 PM ET

S&P 500 and Dow close in correction as the AI rout deepens

The S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, its seventh straight loss and longest streak since February 2020, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73, the Nasdaq 100 fell 5.61% to 25,028.98, the Dow fell 1.93% to 48,147.33 and the Russell 2000 fell 2.8% to 2,581.79. For the week the S&P 500 lost 8.6%, its worst week since April 2025, the Nasdaq lost 10%, and the Nasdaq 100 had its worst week since March 2020 (Reuters), the Dow lost 8.0% and the Russell 2000 lost 7.5%. The Nasdaq is now down 2.7% for 2026, while the S&P 500 (+0.9%) and Dow (+0.2%) are barely positive. The S&P 500 closed about 11.6% below its October 6 record close of 7,818.93, and the Dow about 11.4% below its August record of 54,349.12. Why it matters: even the equal weight S&P 500 fell 1.98%, so the selling is no longer limited to a few mega cap names. AMD fell about 13% and Broadcom led chip losses.

Fresh · Fri 20 Nov close, with Tue 17 Nov results as context

Nvidia falls 14% on Friday despite blowout results; Reuters reports an SEC accounting inquiry

Nvidia closed at $125.53, down $20.45 or 14.01%, about 23% below its $162.85 close a week earlier (calculated). On Tuesday it reported Q3 revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and above the roughly $108.8 billion expected, and guided Q4 to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, with gross margin of 71% to 72% versus 74.8% in Q3. On Friday Reuters, citing two people briefed on the matter, reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia, and the SEC declined to comment. The stock was down about 7% before that report and closed down about 14%, and it is unclear how much of the loss the report caused. Why it matters: good numbers did not stop the slide, which points to doubts about how long AI spending can grow and about governance, not about last quarter’s sales. The report is unconfirmed and not a finding.

Context · Wed 18 Nov, 2:00 PM ET, plus Fri 20 Nov close

Fed minutes stay hawkish while Treasuries rally on the selloff

Minutes of the October 27 to 28 meeting, where the Fed held at 3.75% to 4.00% in a 10 to 2 vote (Cook and Hammack wanted a hike), said almost all officials judged more hikes would likely be appropriate, and a couple floated a move larger than 25 bp (basis points, 0.25% each) in December. The next meeting is December 8 to 9, and a 25 bp hike was priced at about 95% to 97% midweek. Even so, the 10 year Treasury yield closed Friday near 4.92%, down about 13 bp on the day from 5.05%, with its biggest weekly fall since early April 2025, as bonds took the flight to safety. Why it matters: yields are falling because of fear, not because the Fed is easing, so stocks lose on both growth and rate worries. October retail sales (Nov 17) rose 0.2% overall and 0.6% excluding autos and gas, above the 0.4% expected.

Fresh · Thu 19 to Sat 21 Nov reports

Houthis hit Riyadh and reports say the US and Israel weigh new Iran strikes

A Houthi missile struck the PIF Tower in Riyadh on Thursday, damaging several floors, and Saudi authorities said no one was hurt. The Saudi led coalition said it shot down three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh early Saturday, and says it struck Hodeidah port and Sanaa, while the Houthis threatened Jeddah and a Saudi power station. CBS reported on Friday, citing sources, that the US and Israel are considering new strikes on Iran in the near term, though a CBS live headline also quotes Trump saying his next move may not come until 2027. Iran’s foreign minister said its 48 hour Hormuz proposal is still on the table, and UKMTO reported a tanker fire after a projectile strike near Hormuz. Why it matters: Brent settled up 0.64% at $92.57 and WTI (January) up 1.2% at $83.41, but both are set for a third weekly decline on strong Middle East exports and rising US inventories. Signals are mixed, so headline risk stays high.

India

Fresh · Fri 20 Nov close, 3:30 PM IST

Nifty and Sensex hit nine month closing highs as India ignores the global rout

The Nifty 50 rose 286.30 points (1.12%) to 25,800.60 and the Sensex rose 897.96 points (1.08%) to 83,919.71, after the Sensex had been down more than 600 points early and the Nifty below 25,500. Nifty Bank rose 2.49%, Nifty IT 1.30% and the Nifty Midcap 100 0.66%, while the Smallcap 100 ended marginally lower. Why it matters: investors are treating India as a place to hide from the AI rout, led by banks and IT, but the rally depends on foreign money (next card).

Fresh · Fri 20 Nov, provisional exchange data

FIIs buy ₹10,308 crore, a 15 session buying streak

FIIs (foreign institutional investors) were net buyers of ₹10,308.42 crore on Friday (gross buys ₹23,596.40 crore, sells ₹13,287.98 crore) while DIIs (domestic institutional investors) were net sellers of ₹4,859.80 crore. FII buying has now run for 15 sessions (14 through Thursday, when FIIs bought ₹7,411.41 crore). Over the five sessions to Friday FIIs bought about ₹32,647 crore (calculated from daily figures). Foreign portfolio investors have put a record roughly $12.2 billion into Indian equities in November, after more than $60 billion of outflows between September 2024 and October 2026 (HDFC Sky). Why it matters: foreign flows are the swing factor if the global selloff turns into forced selling elsewhere. Provisional figures can be revised.
