Global Macro
United States
June CPI Drops 0.4% — Biggest Monthly Fall Since April 2020
Headline inflation fell sharply to 3.5% year-over-year in June, down from 4.2% in May, beating economist forecasts of 3.8%. Core CPI (which strips out food and energy) came in flat on the month at 2.6% annually, versus an expected 2.9%. The primary driver was a 5.7% monthly plunge in energy prices, thanks to the partial ceasefire in the US-Iran conflict easing oil supply fears. Why it matters: softer CPI reduces the urgency for the Fed to hike rates aggressively, but Fed Chair Kevin Warsh cautioned Congress that "mission accomplished" is not yet his view. Markets now price a roughly 50% chance of a September rate hike — down from higher levels — which drove equities and gold higher on Tuesday.
Big Bank Earnings: Goldman Surges 7.9%, IBM Craters 25%
Q2 earnings season launched in force on July 14. Goldman Sachs beat expectations and rose 7.9%, JPMorgan added 1.5%, and Bank of America gained 1.3%. On the other side, Citigroup dropped 5.8% and Wells Fargo fell 3.3% on mixed results. IBM collapsed 25% after warning that Q2 profits would undershoot due to soft demand in software and infrastructure. Why it matters: financials broadly beat, confirming resilient profit margins even in a high-rate environment. IBM's warning is a signal that enterprise IT spending is bifurcating — AI infrastructure wins, legacy software loses.
Geopolitical Alert
Trump Replaces Hormuz Fee With Gulf Investment Deals — Tensions Remain
Trump reversed his 20% shipping levy on Strait of Hormuz traffic, replacing it with investment deal commitments from Gulf states. The US military carried out a third consecutive night of strikes against Iran on Monday, and tankers were attacked in the Strait. Oil remains elevated. Why it matters: this is not a de-escalation — it is a policy pivot that keeps energy markets on edge. Any resumption of Iranian counter-attacks or shipping disruptions would immediately spike oil and reignite the inflation trade.
India
India Retail Inflation Breaches RBI's 4% Target at 4.38% in June
India's CPI inflation rose to 4.38% in June — the first breach of the RBI's 4% upper comfort threshold since January 2025. The primary driver is energy import costs driven by elevated global crude prices. Simultaneously, FIIs turned net sellers (outflows of ~Rs 739 crore) after an eight-session buying streak. DIIs absorbed Rs 2,927 crore, cushioning the fall. Why it matters: India's equity market is directly exposed to crude — as a large oil importer, rising Brent is a double tax: it hurts fiscal math and keeps the RBI from cutting rates.
HCL Tech Falls 4.4% Despite Beat — Guidance Unchanged
HCL Technologies posted Q1 FY27 results that beat expectations on revenue and profit, but maintained its full-year guidance unchanged — a signal of caution despite resilient demand. The stock was the top Sensex laggard, down 4.4%. Wipro's Q1 IT revenue is expected to fall 1.7% quarter-on-quarter due to wage hikes and acquisition costs. Why it matters: India's IT sector, the market's anchor, is navigating a dual headwind of elevated wages and client budget scrutiny. An unchanged guidance from HCL in this environment reads as conservatism, not confidence.
Europe
ECB on Hold; EU-US Trade Deal Holds at 15% Tariff Cap
The ECB is expected to hold rates at its July 23 meeting after hiking in June. The EU-US trade deal agreed in late June caps US tariffs on European exports at 15%, and the EU removed tariffs on US industrial goods. However, EU-China trade tensions are rising. Why it matters: rate stability in Europe removes a key risk for the euro and European equities, but the widening EU-China rift on trade imbalances creates a fresh source of market uncertainty, particularly for manufacturing-heavy German and French indices.
China
China June Exports Surge 27% — Fastest Pace Since October 2021
China's June exports rose 27% year-on-year in dollar terms, beating estimates of 18.2%. Imports jumped 36%, the largest gain since June 2021, and the trade surplus hit $125.6 billion. Shanghai Composite closed 2.15% higher on Tuesday. Today, markets watch China Q2 GDP, with retail sales expected to show continued domestic weakness (falling year-on-year) while industrial output holds at around +4.6% year-on-year. Why it matters: China's export engine is running hot partly due to a tariff rush — front-loading shipments before new restrictions hit. The domestic consumption picture remains weak, which is a structural drag that no amount of export data can mask.
APAC
Nikkei Extends Rally on CPI Relief; KOSPI Up 7.67% on SK Hynix Euphoria
Japan's Nikkei 225 rose about 1% today tracking Wall Street's CPI-driven rally, with tech and chip stocks leading — Kioxia +6.7%, Advantest +4.4%. South Korea's KOSPI surged 7.67% as of last close, reflecting the broader recovery in semiconductor stocks following SK Hynix's Nasdaq IPO success and HBM chip demand remaining robust. Singapore's Q2 GDP came in at 5.7%, beating expectations, driven by strong manufacturing. Why it matters: the APAC recovery post-Iran shock is sharp and concentrated in AI hardware names. This validates the structural AI capex cycle thesis, but also flags elevated volatility risk if US-Iran tensions re-escalate.

AI Watch
Infrastructure Deal
NVIDIA and SK Hynix Sign Multiyear Partnership for Next-Gen AI Memory
NVIDIA and SK Hynix announced a multiyear co-development deal spanning Vera Rubin AI supercomputers, RTX Spark PCs, and Jetson Thor robotics platforms. SK Hynix will also use NVIDIA tools for semiconductor simulation and digital twin factory operations. This comes just after SK Hynix's landmark $26.5B Nasdaq ADR listing — the largest foreign company listing in US history. Strategic importance: this is not a transactional deal — it's a deep vertical integration play that locks SK Hynix into NVIDIA's AI infrastructure roadmap. For investors, it signals that the AI hardware capex cycle has legs beyond current spending patterns.
Regulation
China AI Companion Law Forces Doubao and Qwen Agent Shutdowns by July 15
China's new AI companion regulation mandates that Doubao and Qwen shut down personalized AI agents effective today. This is part of a broader regulatory tightening around emotionally responsive AI systems in China. Strategic importance: Chinese AI regulation is diverging sharply from US policy, creating a bifurcated global AI development environment. For multinationals, products designed for China must now strip personalization features — a meaningful engineering and business cost.
Market Shift
Chinese AI Models Now Serve 45% of OpenRouter Traffic — Up From 2% One Year Ago
Xiaomi's MiMo-V2-Pro now handles 21.1% of OpenRouter traffic (4.21 trillion weekly tokens) versus OpenAI's 7.5%. Chinese AI providers collectively serve 45% of global API routing traffic, a dramatic reversal from under 2% a year ago. The driver is cost and availability, not benchmark performance. Strategic importance: this is the most significant competitive shift in the AI infrastructure layer in 2026. The commoditization of AI model access, driven by ultra-cheap Chinese alternatives, compresses margins for US frontier labs and accelerates adoption timelines globally.
Key Takeaways
01
The June US CPI beat is real but the inflation story is not over. Energy fell 5.7% in the month, which is the mechanical reason headline CPI looked good. But oil is back above $80 (WTI) and $84 (Brent) because the Iran ceasefire has broken down. If crude holds or rises from here, July CPI will reverse course — and the Fed will face a choice between hiking into a slowing economy or letting inflation re-accelerate. Watch the next 60 days of oil pricing as the leading indicator of Fed policy direction.
02
India is caught in a geopolitical trap it cannot trade its way out of. As a major oil importer, every dollar of Brent above $80 is inflationary for India — and the RBI cannot respond with rate cuts if India's own CPI is above target (4.38% in June). The RBI is boxed in: cutting would weaken the rupee and worsen imported inflation; holding kills domestic credit growth. Until Hormuz tensions durably ease, India's monetary policy remains frozen.
03
The AI chip cycle is showing two separate stories simultaneously: euphoric hardware demand (SK Hynix, NVIDIA, HBM memory) versus deteriorating AI application spending (IBM's 25% crash on soft software demand, IBM's warning about "AI hyperscaler budget caution"). The market is right to bifurcate — infrastructure-layer AI capex is real and durable; enterprise application software spending is being reviewed. Position accordingly: long the semiconductor supply chain, cautious on legacy enterprise IT vendors pivoting to AI.
04
China's trade data is structurally deceptive. Exports up 27% and imports up 36% sounds like a booming economy — but both figures are inflated by a tariff-rush dynamic where companies front-loaded shipments before new US and EU restrictions take effect. Retail sales remain weak year-on-year. The Politburo meeting in late July is the event to watch: if stimulus announcements disappoint, the domestic consumption gap will re-emerge as a market narrative and drag Asian equities in August.
05
In the next 24 to 72 hours: China Q2 GDP (today) is the critical data point — watch whether domestic activity beats or misses the muted expectations. The Bank of Canada rate decision (July 15) may offer clues on whether commodity-linked central banks are shifting dovish. Fed Chair Warsh's next communication and any Hormuz re-escalation will set the tone for whether the July 14 equity rally holds or reverses into week-end.
