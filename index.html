01 — Global Macro
United States
Fed Minutes Keep Year-End Rate Hike on the Table
All 19 Fed officials backed September's 25bp hike to 3.75–4.00%, the first increase since July 2023. Most participants assessed another hike would be appropriate by year end. Several officials said rates are not yet restrictive. October hike odds remain slim at 17–20%, with futures pointing to December.
Why it matters: The Fed is confirming a structurally higher-for-longer rate regime. A December hike would push real rates further into restrictive territory just as earnings season begins, threatening equity multiples and adding pressure to the long end of the yield curve.
10-Year Treasury Yield Touches 5.36% — Highest Since April 2002
The 10-year Treasury hit 5.36% intraday before closing at 5.28%. The 30-year hit 5.73%, its highest since May 2002. A $39bn 10-year auction drew solid demand, pulling yields off session highs. Barclays raised its year-end 10-year forecast to 5.25%. The 30-year mortgage rate climbed to 7.49%.
Why it matters: At 5.28%, the 10-year now directly competes with equities for capital. BofA's Savita Subramanian called it the first genuine bond-vs-equity rivalry in decades. Small caps and rate-sensitive sectors face the most pressure; the Russell 2000 fell 1.3% on Tuesday alone.
Initial Jobless Claims Released at 8:30 AM ET Today
The Department of Labor's weekly jobless claims report for the week ending October 3 is the primary macro catalyst for Thursday's session. Prior reading: 197K (below 201K expected), confirming a resilient labor market. Equity futures were flat-to-mixed pre-open, with bearish session bias per analyst dashboards.
Why it matters: A low print cements the case for another Fed hike and keeps the dollar bid. A weak print could ease rate-hike pricing, giving relief to Treasuries and small caps. Either way, this is the number that decides today's tape.
India
Nifty and Sensex Consolidating Around 22,600 and 72,400 Levels
The Nifty 50 closed around 22,600–22,620 for the week ending October 1, with the Sensex near 72,480. Bank Nifty has been resilient, up ~0.58% as financials provide a buffer. Nifty Auto declined 2.6% on fuel-cost concerns. Gift Nifty signaled a flat-to-cautious open this week tracking US cues.
Why it matters: India's benchmarks have reset significantly from their 26,000 highs (October 2025). The market is in a consolidation phase, with foreign institutional investors (FIIs) cautious amid a strong dollar and rising US yields, which erodes the relative attractiveness of EM equities including India.
India Rupee Weakens as Dollar Surges to 18-Month High
The Indian rupee fell 0.3% against the dollar, mirroring weakness across emerging market currencies. The Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index rose 0.3%, with the Euro dropping to $1.12 and almost all G10 currencies weaker. The MSCI EM equities index fell 1%. South Korea's Kospi dropped 2% in its worst session this month.
Why it matters: A stronger dollar means imported inflation for India (especially oil), pushes FII outflows and weakens the rupee further, creating a feedback loop. With Brent near $100, India's current account will face pressure and RBI may be forced to defend the rupee through forex reserves.
Europe
⚠ Geopolitical: Iran Escalates Hormuz Attacks — Brent Briefly Tops $102
At least nine tanker attacks were recorded in the Strait of Hormuz so far in October according to UK officials. Houthis struck two Saudi airports in Riyadh and Abha, killing three. Supertanker rates hit a record $77M to ship US crude to Asia versus a 2025 average of $9.2M. A US blockade of Iranian ports has near-halted Iranian crude exports. Brent briefly topped $102 before settling at $100.88.
Why it matters: Energy is Europe's primary vulnerability. Eurozone CPI and inflation expectations are directly tied to energy prices. The ECB's current rate path — already under pressure from the ECB's Schnabel signaling hikes — becomes even more hawkish if oil stays above $100, which is deeply contractionary for a European economy already dealing with slow growth and a weakening euro at $1.12.
ECB Leans Hawkish — Schnabel Signals Rates Have Reached the Floor
ECB Executive Board member Isabel Schnabel stated publicly that interest rates have reached the bottom and expressed comfort with market bets on a rate hike. This was the first time an ECB policymaker declared rates have hit a floor rather than are merely in a "good place." The euro fell 0.5% to $1.12, under pressure from energy prices and France's fiscal situation.
Why it matters: Markets are now pricing ECB rate hikes rather than cuts. This reversal in the ECB's easing cycle, combined with energy shocks and a weaker euro, complicates the Eurozone growth picture considerably. European equities face a double-squeeze from higher rates and energy costs.
China
Fortescue's Iron Ore Sales Miss on CMRG Negotiations — Beijing Demand Signal
Fortescue (ASX: FMG) reported iron ore shipments down 6% to 46.8Mt for the September quarter. Crucially, sales of 42.9Mt fell 3.9Mt short of shipments due to ongoing negotiations with China Mineral Resources Group (CMRG). Hematite realised price was $80/dmt, or 82% of the Platts 61% index. Net debt tripled to $2.8bn.
Why it matters: CMRG's negotiating posture is a proxy for China's demand appetite for steel-making inputs. A standoff suggests Chinese steel mills are price-sensitive and production is subdued — consistent with China's broader slowdown narrative. For Indian markets, this matters as Chinese stimulus expectations are the key swing factor in global metals.
China Domestic Demand Recovery Under Stress as Stimulus Focus Remains
China's Politburo signaled it will maintain "proactive" fiscal and "moderately loose" monetary policy. Full-year 2026 GDP growth is tracking around 4.2–4.8% according to multiple sell-side estimates, with US tariff pressure and weak domestic demand the primary headwinds. China-EU trade tensions remain elevated, with Beijing pushing back against EU procurement market concerns.
Why it matters: China's ability to fire a credible stimulus bazooka is the most important optionality in global macro. If domestic demand doesn't recover, commodity markets (copper, iron ore, oil) remain under structural selling pressure, weighing on EM growth and India's export peers.

Japan 30-Year Bond Yield Hits Record 4%, Signals Fiscal Stress

South Korea's Kospi declined 2% in its worst single session this month, dragged by EM-wide risk-off and dollar strength.
Why it matters: Japan's long-end yield pressure signals global bond markets are undergoing a structural repricing. The BoJ's slow exit from yield curve control, combined with a weak yen and rising JGB yields, creates a carry-trade unwind risk — one of the few tail risks large enough to move global markets rapidly.

03 — AI Watch
Infrastructure Deal
Google Begins Paying SpaceX $920M/Month for 110,000 Nvidia GPUs — Starting October 2026
The $920M/month compute deal between Google and SpaceX — announced before SpaceX's IPO — officially enters full-rate payment as of October 2026 and runs through June 2029, implying ~$30 billion total. The deal gives Google access to approximately 110,000 Nvidia GPUs, CPUs, and memory for its Gemini Enterprise agent platform. SpaceX also holds a separate $1.25B/month deal with Anthropic. SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen says the company is on track for $100B ARR, with a new hosting deal adding ~$13B ARR from December 1. Reports suggest SpaceX is now seeking an additional $40B in debt — split $10B in bank loans and $30B in IG bonds — to buy more Nvidia chips, with Apollo leading and PIMCO considering the deal.

Strategic importance: SpaceX is becoming the most consequential node in the AI infrastructure supply chain — simultaneously a GPU reseller, hyperscaler, and launch provider. This creates a concentration risk for AI compute: Google and Anthropic's frontier model pipelines now depend partly on SpaceX's ability to procure and manage Nvidia's most advanced chips. For equity markets, this further cements Nvidia's pricing power and validates a long AI capex supercycle.
Market Event
SpaceX Stock Falls 2.5% on $40B Debt Financing Plans for Nvidia Chips
News that SpaceX is looking to raise $40 billion in debt — primarily to purchase Nvidia chips — weighed on SpaceX shares (-2.5%) even as its business fundamentals remain strong. SpaceX's Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 data centres in Memphis are already generating billions per month in recurring AI compute revenue. Analyst Dan Ives (Yorkville Ives) maintains an Outperform with a $225 target citing Starlink cash flow and the AI backlog. Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas called the stock "cheap" ahead of the Starship 15 launch in late October.

Strategic importance: The AI infrastructure build-out is entering a debt-financed phase. Levering up to buy GPUs signals that compute demand is far outpacing equity financing capacity. This is a meaningful shift in AI market structure — from venture-funded to investment-grade-bond-funded — and has implications for credit markets and Nvidia's long-term demand visibility.
Nuclear + AI Power
Google Signs 3.6GW Nuclear Power Deal with Constellation Energy — Uranium Stocks Volatile
Google signed a 20-year power deal with Constellation Energy for 3.6GW of nuclear capacity to power its AI data centres. This triggered a uranium sector rally on Wednesday, which was entirely reversed Thursday as the Global X Uranium ETF fell 4.47% in a broad commodity selloff. ASX-listed uranium names including Paladin, Deep Yellow, and Boss Energy all fell 5–8%.

Strategic importance: The nuclear-AI nexus is becoming a genuine structural theme. AI's power demands are so large that cloud hyperscalers are now signing decades-long nuclear supply agreements. This is fundamentally bullish for uranium and nuclear operators over the medium term, even if near-term price action is volatile due to dollar strength and commodity ETF outflows.
04 — Key Takeaways
1
The 5% yield era is back — and this time the Fed is hiking into it. The US 10Y at 5.28% is not noise. It is a structural repricing. When bond yields rise to these levels, equity P/E multiples compress — especially for high-multiple tech and rate-sensitive sectors. Watch the S&P 500 equal-weight index (-0.81% on Tuesday) rather than the cap-weighted index as the true health indicator. The real market breadth is deteriorating even as the headline holds near record highs because of a handful of megacap tech names.
2
Oil near $100 is the inflation ticking clock. Brent briefly topping $102 on Hormuz attacks is not just an energy story. It is a CPI story, a Fed story, and a global growth story simultaneously. A sustained oil price above $95–100 makes a December Fed hike near-certain, raises inflation expectations (already at 3.9% for 1-year), and pressures India's current account and rupee. The geopolitical trigger (Iran + Houthis) has no near-term off-switch.
3
The dollar rally is the hidden killer for EM assets including India. DXY at 102.49 — an 18-month high — is systematically compressing EM equities, commodities, and currencies. India's Nifty has already reset from 26,000 to 22,600. If the dollar holds above 102 through the Fed's December decision, Indian markets face continued FII outflows, rupee pressure, and imported inflation. Watch the RBI's next response carefully — they have already hiked rates once in the past year.
4
AI infrastructure has entered a debt-financed supercycle — watch credit spreads. SpaceX's planned $40bn debt raise to buy Nvidia GPUs is a watershed moment. AI capex is now large enough that it requires investment-grade bond markets, not just equity. If this works — and Apollo and PIMCO step in — it validates a sustained demand floor for Nvidia. If credit spreads widen or the deal struggles, it signals a capex cycle turning point. This is the most important structural AI market signal to track in Q4 2026.
5
Next 24–72 hours: Jobless Claims (today 8:30 ET), PepsiCo earnings (today), and CPI data (October 14) are the three pivotal catalysts. If claims come in low again (<200K), expect the 10Y to test 5.40% and equity futures to slide. PepsiCo will be the first window into consumer resilience under $100 oil and high rates. And the October 14 CPI print — the first post-September-hike data — will determine whether the Fed pulls the trigger in December or waits. JPMorgan, Goldman, Wells Fargo, Citi, and UnitedHealth all report October 13. That week will define Q4 2026 market direction.
