United States
July CPI Prints In-Line at 3.4% — Fed Rate Hike Bets Fall Below 50%
The Bureau of Labor Statistics released July CPI on August 12 at 3.4% year-on-year, down from 3.5% in June and matching the consensus forecast exactly. Monthly CPI rose 0.1%, while core CPI (excluding food and energy) came in at 2.5% annually and 0.2% month-on-month, also in-line. Gasoline fell 2.9%, which absorbed much of the energy-related inflation from the Iran war. The in-line print removes immediate pressure for the Federal Reserve to hike at its September 15-16 meeting, with money markets now pricing a less-than-50% probability of a September hike. This matters because elevated Fed rate expectations had been driving yields higher and compressing equity multiples throughout August.
Cisco Q4 Beat-and-Raise but Stock Sells Off in After-Hours — AI Infrastructure Narrative Under Scrutiny
Cisco reported fiscal Q4 2026 results after market close on August 12, beating consensus on both revenue and EPS and raising forward guidance, with AI orders reaching $9 billion for the full year versus the original target of $5 billion. Despite the headline beat, shares sold off after hours because the market had set expectations even higher — particularly on FY27 revenue growth guidance and whether management would signal a push beyond $9 billion in AI orders. The disconnect between fundamentals and the market reaction is a warning sign: when a beat-and-raise quarter fails to reward a stock up 57% year-to-date, valuations have priced in an optimistic future that execution must consistently meet.
Intel Raises $20 Billion in Equity Offering — Foundry Bet Draws Confidence from Wall Street
Intel announced a $20 billion equity raise, framed as a conviction bet on AI chip foundry demand. While the offering dilutes existing shareholders by roughly 5%, analysts at Bank of America and UBS viewed it positively, citing it as an endorsement of Intel's confidence in its manufacturing roadmap and a removal of an overhang on the stock. Intel closed at $97.71 before the deal. The raise is strategically significant because Intel is competing against TSMC and Samsung in advanced chip manufacturing, and capital scale is the single largest barrier to entry in that race.
India
India CPI Hits 19-Month High at 4.45% in July — RBI Rate Hike Possible by December
India's retail inflation rose to 4.45% in July, up from 4.38% in June, marking the highest level in 19 months. Food inflation accelerated to 5.52%, with rural areas seeing 5.79% food price growth. Southern states led the charts, with Telangana at 6.32% and Karnataka at 4.9%. The data came in below the 4.5% forecast, so markets did not react sharply, but the trajectory is clear: RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra has signalled inflation will peak in Q3 FY27. Morgan Stanley now expects the RBI to begin a 75 basis point hiking cycle starting December. For Indian equities, this means the interest rate environment that provided a tailwind in 2025 is turning, and rate-sensitive sectors like real estate and banking face increasing headwinds into year-end.
Tata Conglomerate Under Pressure as N. Chandrasekaran Steps Down Ahead of AGM
Tata Sons Chairman N. Chandrasekaran's sudden resignation ahead of the Tata conglomerate's annual general meeting scheduled for August 18 created significant market anxiety. TCS fell 3.7% on August 12, acting as the single largest drag on the Sensex. The timing of the resignation, just days before the AGM, has raised questions about internal succession and governance at India's most valuable corporate group. For investors, uncertainty at the holding company level in a conglomerate of this size and sectoral breadth is a meaningful risk event, not just a corporate story.
Nifty Opens Cautiously on August 13 — Gift Nifty Signals Range-Bound Session

DII inflows have been cushioning the decline, but the combination of rising domestic inflation, a weak rupee, and high oil creates a challenging macro backdrop for Indian equities through the September quarter.
Europe
ECB at 2.25% Deposit Rate — September Hike Now 78% Market-Implied as Energy Inflation Persists
The ECB hiked its deposit facility rate to 2.25% at its June 11 meeting, citing the renewed inflation shock from higher energy costs linked to the US-Iran war. Its next meeting is September 10. Markets currently price a 78% probability of another 25 basis point hike at that meeting. The ECB's June projections revised up inflation for 2026 and 2027, while cutting the growth forecast for 2026 to just 0.8%. This is a textbook stagflation dilemma for the Governing Council: hiking to fight inflation risks crushing an already weak economy. European equities are caught between a tightening central bank and fading consumer demand.

China's economy underperformed in Q2, partly attributed to the energy disruption from the Iran war affecting industrial output. The PBOC holds its monthly policy meeting this weekend, with growing market expectations for liquidity support after Q2 fell below its 5% growth target. Slightly more than half of economists in a Bloomberg poll now expect no cut in 2026, as the PBOC prefers targeted interventions over broad rate cuts. China's Hang Seng rose 1% on August 11 on optimism ahead of the weekend meeting, and hyperscaler results from Tencent provided a positive AI-linked boost. The strategic risk for global markets is that a Chinese growth shortfall compounds weak European demand, leaving the US as the sole engine of the global economy.

Meta Unveils Muse Code on Muse Spark 1.2 — Enterprise Coding AI at $1.25/M Tokens
Meta launched Muse Code, a coding AI built on its Muse Spark 1.2 model, priced at $1.25 per million input tokens and $4.25 per million output tokens. Regulation
Europe AI Transparency Rules Live — AI Systems Must Now Identify Themselves to Users
Effective August 2, Europe's continent-wide AI transparency requirements came into force, requiring all AI systems that interact with humans to identify themselves as AI. This is the first binding disclosure requirement of this kind at continental scale, and it applies from the UK to Southeast Asian offices of European companies. For software companies, financial institutions, and customer service platforms using AI, this triggers immediate compliance costs and workflow changes. Longer term, it sets a precedent that markets should watch: other major jurisdictions — including India and the US — are evaluating similar mandates, which would reshape the economics of enterprise AI deployment globally.
