Fresh · weekend reports to Mon 16 Nov

Iran says tankers hit mines, but the US disputes it and flows look near normal

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers struck mines on an unauthorised route through the strait on Sunday, one of them a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude, which sank, and Iran’s regular navy said another tanker hit a mine east of the strait late Sunday; Iranian media reported five tankers catching fire in 48 hours. These are Iranian claims; no one else has independently confirmed them, and CENTCOM says the strait has been swept clear and the main shipping routes are clear. Separately, Iran launched six drones at a US air defence radar at Bahrain’s Al Rifaa helicopter base on Sunday, which Bahrain said its defences intercepted (Guardian). President Trump said Thursday that the US no longer needs a deal and shut down Vice President Vance’s negotiating effort (Washington Post), while Reuters, citing three sources, says he may hold off new strikes if Iran and the Houthis stop provoking the US. Why it matters: Reuters reported Monday that Hormuz shipments are nearly back to pre war levels, and the IEA’s November report points to a supply overhang of roughly 2 million barrels a day this quarter (NDTV Profit). Headline risk is high, but a confirmed supply loss is not yet in the data.
