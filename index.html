The oil shock is the single most important macro variable right now. WTI topping $90 with Hormuz mining confirmed is not a temporary spike — it is a structural supply constraint. Every $10 increase in oil adds roughly 0.3–0.5pp to US CPI. At this trajectory, inflation could re-accelerate into Q4, removing the Fed's option to pause even if the labor market softens. 02
Synchronised global tightening is back. Fed, ECB, and BOJ are all leaning toward rate hikes in September simultaneously. This combination has not occurred in this cycle. Historically, synchronised tightening compresses risk premiums, strengthens the dollar structurally, and creates sharp reversals in carry trades. EM currencies — including the rupee — face headwinds regardless of domestic policy.

Gold's failure to rally on genuine geopolitical risk is a tell. With US-Iran exchanging strikes and Hormuz under threat, gold should theoretically be surging. Instead it fell 1.86%. The reason: rate hike expectations are overpowering safe-haven demand. This is the same dynamic seen in early 2022 when rate fears beat Ukraine geopolitics. Gold will only reassert itself if the Fed pivots or real yields peak — neither is imminent.

The rupee is being defended at 95/dollar. Any sustained oil move toward $100 would force either fiscal pressure on subsidies or pass-through to CPI, which the RBI cannot easily accommodate given global tightening. FII flows remain cautious — Q3 volatility is the base case.
