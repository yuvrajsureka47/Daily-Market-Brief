Fresh · Tue 17 Nov to Wed 18 Nov early hours

US strikes an Iranian missile site after drone attacks on Bahrain

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said they targeted a US radar at Bahrain’s Isa Air Base. CENTCOM said the drones were intercepted without damage, and Bahrain said its air defences destroyed them. US fighter jets then struck an IRGC missile launch site on Iran’s Qeshm Island “in self defense,” which Trump described as limited. Early Wednesday, Kuwait and Bahrain both reported intercepting further drones. A third US carrier group has arrived in the region. Why it matters: US officials describe the strikes as defensive, not a major escalation, and oil reacted modestly: Brent settled up 0.76% at $93.14 and WTI up 0.34% at $86.01. Both are still down sharply for November (Brent about 20%, WTI about 16%, per Kitco) as Hormuz flows recover and the IEA sees a surplus.

Earnings

Nvidia beats and guides far above forecasts in its first report without Huang

Fresh · Tue 17 Nov, results released after the close
Third quarter revenue was $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and 20% from the prior quarter, against about $109.1 billion expected, with data center revenue of $107.6 billion (+110%). Non-GAAP earnings per share were $2.68 against about $2.47 to $2.48 expected, and Nvidia guided fourth quarter revenue to $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, against roughly $125 billion expected. The outlook assumes no China data center compute revenue. On the call, board chair Harvey Jones said the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” and interim CEO Colette Kress said the company is no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center chips to China. The Wall Street Journal reported that the SEC has opened a civil investigation into Nvidia’s China business, which the company did not address, and the board has set up a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee. The stock closed at $155.46 and rose roughly 3% to 5% after hours. Why it matters: the guide eases fears of an AI spending slowdown, but leadership, the investigations and China exposure are unresolved.

Capital Markets

Anthropic prices its IPO tonight with the book six times covered

Fresh · Tue 17 Nov, order book closed; pricing Wed evening ET
Anthropic is offering 825 million Class A shares at a marketed $70 to $78 on the Nasdaq as ANT, raising up to about $64.4 billion at the top, plus a $26 billion private placement from seven technology companies. CNBC reported the order book closed Tuesday afternoon at about six times the shares on offer, and the Financial Times reported that bankers expect to price above the range, which could lift the valuation past $1.5 trillion fully diluted. At the range, the company is valued at about $916 billion to $1.02 trillion on shares outstanding, and up to about $1.4 trillion fully diluted. Pricing is expected Wednesday evening (US Eastern) and first trade Thursday, subject to regulatory clearance. No final price had been filed at the time of writing. Why it matters: pricing above $78 would confirm strong demand even after the selloff, and a price near $70 would signal it has cooled.
