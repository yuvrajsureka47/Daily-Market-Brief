Key Takeaways
01
The US-Iran conflict has not caused an oil supply shock — yet. The US military's active naval escort program and strike operations have kept tanker passage moving through the Strait. But the ceasefire framework is broken, talks are stalled, and four consecutive nights of airstrikes signal this is escalating, not de-escalating. Any significant closure of the Strait re-introduces $100+ crude, and that changes the entire Fed-inflation-equity narrative for H2 2026.
02
Softer US CPI and PPI are doing real work in markets. By taking a near-term Fed hike off the table, the disinflation data is the reason equities can hold at elevated levels despite an active war in the Middle East. The market is essentially betting that the war stays in a box — confined enough not to cause a durable inflation shock. This is a fragile equilibrium. Q2 earnings now need to confirm the growth side of the equation, and IBM's miss shows that earnings-driven selloffs can be brutal even in a broadly positive market.
03
China's GDP miss and South Korea's $880B AI bet tell the same underlying story from different angles. China's domestic economy is hollowing out — manufacturing and exports are strong but consumer spending is dead. South Korea is betting the answer to geopolitical and economic pressure is to own the AI hardware supply chain. Both moves point to a world where AI infrastructure capex is the primary driver of growth investment in East Asia — and where the companies sitting at the center of that capex cycle (memory, data centers, semiconductors) are the long-term winners.
04
India is quietly in its best macro position in years relative to peers. The current account has flipped to surplus, FII inflows of $7B returned following the government stabilisation package, and India VIX at 13.27 signals the domestic market is not particularly scared. The oil bill savings are real and fiscal. However, the direct human exposure — Indian crew members killed in Hormuz tanker attacks — elevates the political dimension and could complicate any future RBI-government messaging on energy risk.
